# Speed experiment

Setup: `O=${COREWAR_ORACLE:-$HOME/.local/share/corewar-theater}`.

## Where it stands (six rounds, the last on 14th November 2026)

Read this and `docs/performance-log.md` before trying anything. Skip ideas rejected there unless something they depended on changed; say what changed.
- The native library is the target since the sixth round (Martin: "Wasm is not important, native performance is what counts"): the library as `make` builds it, `-O3 -fPIC`, the context in caller memory, with gcc and with clang. Keep or reject on those two; the wasm build gets one A/B for the report and must not lose more than a few percent. Rounds one to five tuned for V8: what they rejected "for wasm" with a native gain is open again (their native numbers are `clang -march=native -DM42_FIXED_CONTEXT`, measure anew).
- The inputs decide what you find. Rounds one to five and half of the sixth were measured on one corpus of '88-style warriors (t20, t80, corpus pairs, the bench: 26 to 94 different instruction words executed). The Koenigstuhl hills in `warriors/koenigstuhl` execute 200 to 1,020, and `main` ran 79% of 94nop's instructions in the generic code, at half the speed of an '88 hill. A code review found that, not a benchmark. Measure every engine change on the hills of the Method section, '94 ones first (Martin: "94 style hills are the most important ones, but I want all to be fast").
- Sixth round: the dispatch index in every cell with Huffman trees of compares, 256 cases in the loops and 256 more plus the generic code in `exec_more()`, ranked over 13 mixes; the fields of an instruction loaded one by one; the loop counter hidden from clang: '94 hills +46 to +70% with gcc and +32 to +58% with clang, '88-style mixes +9 to +14% and +6 to +14%. The first half, tuned on the old corpus with 64 cases, had +20% and +27% there and made the '94 hills 20% slower than `main`. gcc and clang are within 5% of each other now.
- What found it: a profile-guided build (`-fprofile-instr-generate`, train, `-fprofile-instr-use`) that is much faster than the plain one says the compiler lacks something the profile has; `perf stat` (loads per simulated instruction: spills) and the loop head in `objdump` say what. Fewer x86 instructions did not mean less time: a computed goto ran 20% fewer for the same cycles.
- The instruction loop of `mars.c` was at a local optimum for V8 and clang's wasm code. In rounds three to five about 4 of 5 changes to it were slower by 1 to 8%, also changes that run fewer instructions. Natively the compilers react as hard: a neutral-looking change moved clang by -5% and gcc by +2% (sixth round). Measure both.
- Exact early ties: the two proofs in `tie_check()` stay; the closed-loop proof of #35 saves 0.7% of t80, less than its traced loop costs in V8. A new proof needs a measured ceiling above 5% first (`bench/tie-ceiling.mjs`). Built in the sixth round and removed again (Martin: 190 lines for a partial slowdown is not a good deal): a static proof for warriors that sit in traps or idle loops (every cell reachable from the queues is no `DAT`, jumps and stores go to fixed places, no needed field is written). It executed 1.7% fewer instructions on t80, 2.7% on 400 corpus pairs and 0.2% on t20, with no wrong tie in 347,000 rounds; in time 0.4 to 0.7% of t80s and 1.7 to 1.9% of the pairs, because the instructions it skips are the cheapest, and 0.1 to 0.3% lost on t20. The patch with it is not in the repository. Tie rounds after their last process death are 17.7% of t80: what is left writes through moving pointers (5%) or has a `DAT` in reach of a conditional (8%). So a new proof has to pay on t20 and the bench too, or be much smaller. To look for one, write an oracle into a scratch copy of `bench/tie-ceiling.c` first: it runs every round to its end and fails when a proof stops a round that is no tie.
- Rejected in wasm and natively: short or ring queues, a denser core, prefetch, tail calls, table dispatch (also on the dense index, and behind 5 levels of the tree), a computed goto per case. More than 256 cases in the loops (clang -4 to -11%), fewer there with the rest behind a call.
- The second hardware thread of a core adds 11% in wasm (22% natively): the code already runs 4.1 instructions per cycle on one thread.
- Open, by expected gain: a worker pool that stays alive for callers with many short runs (worker start is 100 ms for 31 workers, the whole fixed cost of `tools/tournament.mjs`); a native driver with threads (today `tools/tournament.mjs --pmars build/42mars` is the native tournament, a process per pairing: t80 with 31 jobs in 1.99 s against 2.63 s on the wasm engine and 1.91 s for `bench-native` on 160 shards, sixth round; contexts through a pointer cost 1 to 3% natively, 4 to 6% in wasm, so wasm keeps one context at a fixed address, #53).
- Where the time goes, t20, per simulated instruction, natively after the sixth round: gcc 40.6 x86 instructions, 10.3 cycles; clang 40.5 and 10.0 (before it 47.9 / 12.6 and 49.0 / 12.9; wasm before it 61.3 / 14.9). Top-down with gcc: retiring 61%, backend bound 20% (memory 17%), frontend bound 12%, bad speculation 7%. L1 data misses about 0.15 per simulated instruction, 80 to 90% in the core, nearly all L2 hits; memory stalls at most 12% of the slots (prefetch, split value/word arrays and a 6-byte cell did not pay, #6). About 5.8 stack-slot loads and stores and 1 stack check per instruction in `execute2_8000()` (#20): most changes that cut live values in C moved V8's register allocation the wrong way; one head for both queues (fourth round) was the exception. Removing the stack check would give about 1%. Open lead: Liftoff time before V8 tiers up on short runs.
- The native build does not follow the wasm build, and gcc does not follow clang: the fourth round was 4 to 7% faster in wasm and 2 to 3% slower natively. Measure every build you make a claim about.
- Tie rounds are 36% (t20) and 47% (t80) of executed instructions.
- The visualizer runs the watched loop (`bench/bench.mjs --watch`, about 46M/s): one instruction per iteration through the generic operand code. A change there does not touch tournament speed.

## Method

1. Idle machine: `uptime` load below 1 and no other `node`, `clang`, `pmars` or `chrome` process (`ps -eo pid,args`, not `pgrep -f`, see CLAUDE.md). Do not time while builds, tests, pMARS checks or other agents run; ask first if another session may be benchmarking.
2. A/B in the same session, alternating, fresh process per run; never compare with a number from the log or another session:
   - natively, the one that decides: `mkdir $S/main && git archive origin/main lib | tar -x -C $S/main`, then `node bench/ab-native.mjs $S/main/lib lib --input 94nop.in --input tiny.in --input 88.in --input t20.in ... --early-tie --cpu 2 --runs 3` (a version is a copy of `lib/`, or a `mars.c` whose neighbours replace the files of `lib/src`). Inputs: at least 94nop, pspace, tiny, nano, lp, 88 and t20; the hills with `node bench/bench-pairs.mjs warriors/koenigstuhl/<hill> --pairs P --rounds R <the hill's settings> --native-input <file>`, pairs, rounds and settings in the first table of the log's sixth round. It builds with gcc and clang as `make` does and says when the two versions executed different instructions. `--cpu`: a CPU locked to a fixed frequency (Martin locked CPU 2 on 14th November 2026; `lscpu -e` shows its MAXMHZ below the others; ask if it is not): ranges within 0.3%, so 3 runs screen and 5 confirm. It runs a fresh copy of the binary every time: one file of a build was 5% slower than its copies while the machine was in use. Inputs: step "Inputs" below; t80s is every 8th match of t80 (the whole t80 takes 35 s per run).
   - wasm: `git show origin/main:wasm/42mars.wasm > $S/base.wasm` (or copy the build before your change), build the variant, `node bench/ab-wasm.mjs $S/base.wasm wasm/42mars.wasm [--only bench,pairs,t20,t80j31]`. About 15 minutes for all measures: run it in the background with a sentinel (CLAUDE.md, "Running long checks"); it restores `42mars.wasm`, so do not rebuild or test meanwhile.
   - a tool: `git show HEAD:tools/tournament.mjs > tools/zz-base-tournament.mjs` (relative imports), `node bench/ab-tool.mjs tools/zz-base-tournament.mjs tools/tournament.mjs 5 <arguments>`, delete the copy.
   - one build per compiler option, to find what a compiler does wrong: `bench/bench-native.mjs --input t20.in --early-tie --cc gcc --flags "-O3 -fPIC -fno-tree-pre" --cpu 2`.
   - A change to `mars.c` that adds code outside the hot loops: check first that the loops are unchanged (`.claude/rules/wasm.md`, "Keep the hot loops byte-identical"); if they are, a timing is only needed when data addresses moved.
3. Measures (median and range):
   | Name | Command | Runs |
   |---|---|---|
   | bench | `node bench/bench.mjs $O/corpus --wasm-match` (80 to 170 ms, includes V8 warm-up) | 7 |
   | pairs | `node bench/bench-pairs.mjs $O/corpus` (40 random corpus pairs, without early ties) | 7 |
   | native | `node bench/bench-native.mjs $O/corpus --runs 7` | 1 |
   | t20 | `node tools/tournament.mjs $O/sets/t20 --rounds 100 --seed 1 --jobs 1` (stderr: `engine (wasm): N s`) | 7 |
   | t80 | the same on `$O/sets/t80` with `--jobs 16` and `--jobs 31` | 5 each |
   | fixed cost | t80 with `--rounds 1 --jobs 31` (worker start, assembly, compile) | 5 |
   | watch | `node bench/bench.mjs $O/corpus --watch` (only for the watched loop) | 7 |
   - A change to tie detection: `node bench/bench-pairs.mjs $O/corpus --pairs 400 --early-tie` on both builds (`ab-wasm` runs the pairs without early ties); it prints the executed instructions too.
   - `tools/tournament.mjs --jobs N` is N threads, the main thread one of them; results do not depend on N. Scheduling or start-up changes: t80 with 16 and 32 jobs, t20 with 1 and 32 jobs, `$O/corpus --bench $O/corpus/agony.red --rounds 200` with 16 and 32 jobs, t80 with `--rounds 1`, one pairing with `--rounds 1000`, all `--seed 1`; stdout must be byte-identical for every job count.
   - References when re-measuring the summary: `node $O/cwbench/benchpairs.mjs` (warmed up, ours against corewar.dev exMARS: the fair single-thread comparison; the short bench shows 2.1x where warmed up is 1.6x), `node $O/cwbench/split.mjs`, `node $O/cwbench/single.mjs exmars`, `node $O/cwbench/tournament.mjs 31 $O/sets/t80.json`, `node tools/tournament.mjs $O/sets/t80 --rounds 100 --seed 1 --jobs 31 --pmars $O/bin/pmars`, `node $O/cwbench/perpair.mjs $O/sets/t20.json` (pairings where we lose to exMARS).
4. Not timings: the first run of a native binary, and a build with statistics counters (`-DLOOP_STATS`-style), which looked 1.3% and 2.9% faster than the clean code. Time the clean build, alternating, 3 runs or more.
5. Instruction counts are exact and need no idle machine: sweep parameters with many native builds in parallel (`taskset -c <core>` each) and read the counts; time only the one or two candidates left.
6. Keep only if the native library is faster beyond noise with gcc and with clang on the '94 hills (ranges do not overlap), neither compiler gets slower by more than 1% on any input, '88-style ones included, the wasm build does not lose more than 2% (`ab-wasm`), and `engine-check` passes. A gain for one compiler that costs the other is rejected, or goes behind a macro of `platform.h` for that compiler when it is one line (`M42_OPAQUE_CLANG`). Smaller, simpler code wins ties. Otherwise restore and rebuild.
7. Record, also when rejected: one row in the right table of `docs/performance-log.md`: `| idea and why it should help | measure before → after | kept / rejected (reason) |`. For a kept change update the "State" line, and the numbers in `docs/plan.md` (Speed) and `README.md`. The README's chart and its Speed table are one measurement of all engines on the same day (`docs/images/speed.mjs` has the commands): change them only with a new run of every engine, then `node docs/images/speed.mjs`.

## Profiles and the hot words

- Native profile: `node bench/bench-native.mjs <dir or --input file> --keep <dir>` leaves the binary (`clang -O3 -fPIC` on `bench-native.c`, which includes the library; `--cc`, `--flags`) and `input.txt`; then `perf stat -e instructions,cycles,L1-dcache-loads,L1-dcache-load-misses,branch-misses <dir>/bench <dir>/input.txt early-tie` or `perf record`.
- Inputs: `node tools/tournament.mjs $O/sets/t20 --rounds 100 --seed 1 --native-input t20.in`, then `--input t20.in --early-tie` (same instruction count as the wasm tournament); corpus pairs: `bench/bench-pairs.mjs ... --native-input`. `--count <file>` writes executions per instruction word.
- Load addresses (which L1 misses hit the core): `perf mem` finds no event here; use AMD IBS: `perf record -e ibs_op/cnt_ctl=1/ -c 20011 -d <dir>/bench <dir>/input.txt early-tie`, `perf script -F addr,data_src`, match against `nm -S <dir>/bench` (`coreMem`, `queueMem`, `arena`). Failed store-to-load forwards: `ls_bad_status2.stli_other` also counts forwarded loads; the only site worth a change was `ADD.F`/`SUB.F` (log, fourth round).
- Re-ranking the 512 words of `HOT`: `node bench/bench-native.mjs --input <file> --early-tie --count <counts>` per mix (the 13 of the log's sixth round), then `node lib/src/gen-dispatch.mjs --rank <count files>` prints the entries with their shares, which also shape the dispatch trees. Weights between the mixes made no difference (94nop three times, the '88 mixes five times).
