    How to read the theater
    
      
    Core War in short. Two or more programs, the warriors, share one circular memory, the core. Every cell holds one instruction. The warriors take turns, one instruction each. A process dies when it executes a DAT, and a warrior is out when its last process is dead. When nobody wins before the cycle limit, the round is a tie.
    
      
    The core. In the ring layout, address 0 is at the top of the innermost ring, addresses go clockwise and continue on the next ring outward. The grid layout is row by row. The end of the core wraps around to address 0, there is no edge.
    
      
    
        
    Color: the warrior that wrote the cell last. Untouched core is a faint dot.
    
        
    Filled tile: code. Hollow tile: DAT, usually a bomb.
    
        
    Flash: just written. White glow: just executed.
    
        
    Pulsing dot: a process sits here. The one that runs next has a white outline.
    
        
    Footprints of this round (F hides them). Corner brackets: a warrior read the cell, in its color; a scanner leaves a lattice of them. + or − instead of a tile: the last change was only an increment or decrement of a pointer, not a bomb. Lit center: the cell was executed this round.
    
        
    Solid arc: an instruction wrote to that cell. Dashed arc: a jump or a new process. Blue line: a scanner compared those cells.
    
        
    Small ring: a process died. Big ring: a warrior is out.
    
      
    
      
    Common strategies. A stone or bomber throws DAT bombs across the core and hopes to hit. A paper copies itself to new places and runs many processes, so single hits do not kill it. A scanner compares cells to find enemy code before it attacks. An imp is MOV 0, 1: it copies itself one cell ahead, forever. A clear wipes the core cell by cell. The cards on the right guess what each warrior is doing right now.
    
      
    How to learn with it. Press learn: 2 instructions per second, and the box at the bottom explains each one, with tags for the executing cell and its A and B targets. Choose the rules nano · 80 to see the instruction text inside every cell. Hover a cell to see its instruction and its neighbours. What's happening lists hits, deaths and changes in behavior, click an entry to see where it happened.
